# fern style: what the reference actually does
Source: "How an Ancient Mummy Changed History", 11:53, 121 shots (cut detection), viewed as 3 frames per shot.

**Pace:** average shot about 6 s, many 0.5–3 s. Fast inserts (hand, skin, eye) cut against wides.

**Reuse:** about 20 unique 3D setups carry the whole 12 minutes. The same shots come back again and again (the hikers top-down, the two hikers standing, the lab table, the face close-up). Fewer, richer sets, each shot several times.

**Light:** mostly daylight or clean practical light. Scenes are bright and readable: blue-sky glacier, white lab, autumn forest, golden-hour ridge. Night only where the story is at night (the fire). Not everything dark and neon.

**People are the subject.** Full body or medium, clearly doing one readable thing: walking, pointing, phoning, carrying, standing over the body. Faces are shown. Several people in frame interact with a prop (scientists round a table).

**Camera:**
- slow push-ins
- top-down drone holds
- low, ground-level moves with foreground rocks wiping frame
- over-the-shoulder follows
- macro slides across texture with shallow depth of field
- simple and steady; no gimmick racks

**Environments are complete and real-feeling:** a cabin, a forest with a parked car, a lab with cabinets, a mountain cross. Not boxes.

**Graphics:**
- relief maps (3D terrain, labels in wide caps, a route line drawing on)
- small date stamps ("SEPTEMBER 19, 1991")
- condensed stat text beside a subject (1.54 M / 13 KG)
- graphics are sparse and sit on top of real-looking imagery

**Archive:** real footage and interviews are mixed in, graded to match.

**Grade:** teal-orange, lifted blacks, heavy grain, vignette, slight chromatic aberration, 2.35:1.
