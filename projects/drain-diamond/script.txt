The $25 Million Hidden in a Drain
Narration: approximately 981 words, four chapters. Estimated spoken runtime: about 8 min 11 s at 120 wpm.

In March 2011, French police search a house outside Paris for the fifth time. This time, they notice a patch of concrete in a rainwater drain. It's a little lighter than the rest.
They drill through it. Underneath is a plastic container. Inside is a ring set with a thirty-one-carat diamond.
The stone came from Harry Winston, a jeweler near the Champs-Élysées. Armed men had robbed the same shop twice. The first time, they came dressed as painters.

## Chapter One · The Service Door
On the morning of October sixth, 2007, staff are opening the Harry Winston boutique on Avenue Montaigne. Four men in painters' overalls are on the premises. They've used the service entrance. And they're armed.
The men threaten and tie up staff. They force a manager to deactivate alarms and open the safes. In minutes, they take a hundred and twenty watches and three hundred and sixty pieces of jewelry.
On the service stairs, the robbers empty fire extinguishers. According to Le Parisien's reconstruction, it's an attempt to cover their tracks. There are employees who can tell police what happened. There's still a question about how the men got inside.

## Chapter Two · The Front Door
Fourteen months later, the boutique is open for business. It's the fourth of December, 2008. Christmas shoppers are out on the avenue. This time, four men approach the front door. Some are wearing wigs and women's clothes. A guard lets them in.
Upstairs, the disguises come off. The visitors pull handguns on two security staff. One of the men goes up another floor and orders staff and a customer downstairs. Soon, sixteen people are on the floor. Some of the employees are called by name.
The robbers have brought a suitcase. They make staff open display cases and safes. Some of the most valuable pieces are kept in concealed compartments. The men know where to look.
Before they leave, they show the people on the floor a grenade and threaten to booby-trap the exit. A driver picks them up outside. They're gone in less than twenty minutes. Court documents put the count at two hundred and ninety-seven pieces of jewelry and a hundred and four watches. At the trial, the second haul alone is valued at seventy-one million euros.
The insurer offers a million-dollar reward for information leading to the return of the jewels. Detectives have a question of their own. Who knew the shop well enough to tell the men where to go, what to open and whose name to call?

## Chapter Three · A Little Cement
The case lands with the Paris police unit that investigates armed robberies. A tip from another police unit gives them names to work with. Among them is Douadi Yahiaoui, a man known as Doudou. Investigators are also looking at one of Harry Winston's guards, Mouloud Djennad.
A link between the two men runs through a gym. Djennad goes to the same one as Yahiaoui's brother-in-law, Patrick Chiniah. The court will later hear that Chiniah puts them in touch.
Detectives put microphones in Yahiaoui's car. He's careful on the phone. In the car, police hear him talk about carats and Rapaport, a price guide used in the diamond trade. Over months of surveillance, officers watch envelopes change hands through car windows. Le Parisien later reports that one lot of thirty-nine stones brings in more than two hundred thousand euros. The sales give detectives something to act on. If they wait too long, more of the stones could disappear into the trade.
In June 2009, police move in around the time of a deal involving buyers from Israel. They detain more than two dozen people in raids across Paris and its suburbs. One search turns up two pairs of earrings. At a house in Les Pavillons-sous-Bois, there's a hidden compartment beneath a staircase. Police find jewels there, some still on their original Harry Winston stands.
It's Yahiaoui's house. Detectives have a place to look and proof that stolen pieces have been kept there. One stone they've heard about in intercepted conversations is still missing. It's the big stone that keeps coming up on the recordings.
They keep coming back to the house. Le Parisien reports that in one intercepted conversation, Yahiaoui mentioned doing a little cement work. On the fifth search, the officers find the lighter patch in the drain.
The container holds nineteen rings and several earrings. The thirty-one-carat diamond is among them. French reports value that stone alone at about six million euros. The Associated Press puts the value of the whole cache at roughly twenty-five million dollars.
The drain gives the police a haul they can lay out and count. The case still has to account for two armed robberies, two sets of disguises, and a guard who was supposed to protect the shop.

## Chapter Four · The Night Before
In February 2015, eight men go on trial in Paris. Mouloud Djennad admits supplying information to Yahiaoui that helped the robbers. Trial reporting says that in 2007 he let the men in the evening before the raid. They spent the night inside the premises. When staff arrived in the morning, the men were already there.
Yahiaoui acknowledges a role in handling stolen goods. He disputes that he was the mastermind. Prosecutor Sylvie Kachaner tells the court he recruited the robbers, gave instructions and took charge of selling the jewels. Farid Allou admits taking part in both robberies. Another defendant admits his involvement in the second. Two others dispute their involvement, although investigators found traces of their DNA in the boutique.
Before the court deliberates, Djennad tells it he's thinking of his former colleagues at Harry Winston. "I'm ashamed every day," he says.
On February twenty-seventh, the court convicts all eight men. Yahiaoui receives the longest sentence, fifteen years. Allou gets ten. Djennad is sentenced to five years, three of them suspended.
Police can account for the thirty-one-carat diamond down to the patch of concrete that hid it. When the sentences come down, hundreds of the pieces taken from Avenue Montaigne are still missing.
