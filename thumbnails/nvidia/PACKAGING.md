## Description (ready to publish)

On November 8, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang told employees he had been unfaithful to his wife. The same day, Nvidia opened
an independent investigation into whether a foreign intelligence service had targeted him. On November 12, Nvidia
announced that Huang had resigned, with CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO.

This film follows the trail behind the allegation: the 2022 U.S. export controls on advanced AI chips; Megaspeed, the
Singapore cloud company whose reported assets grew from $33 million to $3 billion in a year; the shipping records of
Nvidia hardware reaching Indonesia; and the memo that, according to the New York Times, circulated among senior U.S.
officials claiming that China's Ministry of State Security recruited Alice Huang to gather information on him.

Accuracy note: the espionage claim is an allegation reported by the New York Times from people briefed on intelligence.
It has not been proven or tested in public. Reuters says it could not verify it independently. China's Foreign Ministry
rejects it, Megaspeed denies illicit activity involving chips, and Nvidia's board said it had no reason to believe
company secrets or national security were compromised. Alice Huang could not be reached for comment. As of
November 11 there was no public finding that Megaspeed broke export rules. The shareholder lawsuit's claims are
allegations before a court. All pictures in this film are illustrations generated in software; no real people are
depicted.
