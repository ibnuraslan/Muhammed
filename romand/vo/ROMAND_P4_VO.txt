That makes the arrangement feel like a benefit of knowing him. The man proposing the investment is already part of their lives. They have attended family gatherings with him. They know his parents. They have watched him marry Florence and raise children. The apparent success of his household supplies reassurance.

Emmanuel remembers promised returns of around sixteen percent. Money passes to Romand on the understanding that it will be placed in Switzerland. Instead, he uses entrusted funds to support his own life.

The deception now begins to reinforce itself. People believe he has money because he has a successful career. Their belief persuades them to give him money. Spending it helps him continue to look like a successful professional.

But their savings remain a debt. Whatever he spends on ordinary life is money he may one day be asked to return. His supposed investments cannot produce the missing funds, because he has not made them.

The financial exploitation extends beyond investment offers. Emmanuel also describes a relative with cancer being offered access to supposed special medicines through Romand’s research connections. The prestigious job becomes a way to sell hope to someone who is ill.

By this point, Romand is maintaining more than an impressive biography. Other people are making financial decisions around it. His claims can affect how they plan for retirement, how they use their savings, and what they believe might help a sick relative.

In 1988, his father-in-law, Pierre Crolet, asks for the return of some money he has entrusted to him. Later that year, Pierre suffers a fatal fall at home while Romand is present.

The circumstances subsequently attract suspicion. Romand denies responsibility, and he is not prosecuted over the death. It cannot be presented as an established additional murder. What is established is the financial problem behind the request: money that relatives believe is available has been disappearing into Romand’s life.

Meanwhile, he begins a relationship with Chantal. She also entrusts him with money—nine hundred thousand francs. Eventually, she wants it returned.

A reassuring explanation can delay that conversation. It cannot repay her.
