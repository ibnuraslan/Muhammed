He travels to meet her and offers another account of his powerful connections. They are going to dinner with Bernard Kouchner, a prominent French political and humanitarian figure. The proposed destination is a country house near Fontainebleau.

Romand does not have the friendship he claims. But the invitation is consistent with the man Chantal believes she knows. Important people and privileged access have been part of his story for years.

They drive out of Paris. The journey takes them into the forest. He pretends to have lost his way, stops, and goes to the boot of the car.

This is the encounter from the beginning of the story: the supposed present, the cord, and the tear gas.

Chantal survives. Romand stops the attack and supplies another explanation for his behavior. He claims to be seriously ill. He takes her back and urges her not to tell anyone. There is even a further arrangement concerning the repayment of her money.

After killing five members of his family and attacking a sixth person, he is still making promises he cannot fulfill.

He returns to the house in Prévessin-Moëns, where Florence and the children remain. During the night leading into Monday, January eleventh, he spreads petrol and sets the house on fire. He also takes barbiturates.

Firefighters find him unconscious but alive. He is taken to hospital in Geneva.

Florence and the children cannot be saved. Examination establishes that they were killed before the fire. His parents are also discovered shot dead in their home. What could initially look like a domestic fire now connects two separate crime scenes and five deliberate killings.

Romand survives, and the investigation begins dismantling the professional identity his family has accepted.
