This changes the picture of what might have happened. The remains could have been preserved, buried, retrieved, altered, and buried again. The head and arm could have been substituted during that process.

The team cannot recover every step. It can connect the broken knee to the body and establish that the separation happened before the final burial. The precise moment when each different person became part of the whole remains uncertain.

And the knee is part of a much wider pattern.

Before the roundhouses existed, this ground held a cemetery. Some people were cremated. Small quantities of their burnt bones went into the earth.

Later, someone dug down into those graves.

In one case, a house had been built with its hearth directly over a child’s cremation burial. After that house was taken down, a pit was dug through its old floor to reach the remains.

People were returning to the dead across generations. Sometimes they removed bones. Sometimes they moved them. Human remains also ended up within the later houses’ walls and floors.

The two assembled bodies belong to a place where the dead repeatedly came back into the lives of the people building above them.
