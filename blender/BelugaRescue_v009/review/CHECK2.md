# BelugaRescue v009: check 2 (after fix round 1). Verdict: FAIL, stopped at budget

`review/preview.mp4` is the preview of the exact package (every 5th frame, 270x480, VO). I watched all 7 contact pages of both previews.
The .blend is withheld (not pushed) until PASS. The four audit scripts were not run on this package because of the budget, and must run before delivery.

## What works
- One continuous camera, frames 1–1736, built to the v008 VO word timings (`beats.json`, `BelugaRescue_v009_VO.words.json`). No retime.
- A real CC-BY beluga sculpt (Ghosttisti), rigged here with a spine, flippers and a split-lip jaw. It reads as a beluga with eyes, melon and flippers, and keeps its shape.
- The diver wears real MakeHuman garments (a yellow swim tee and violet leggings, solidified), not a onesie, plus braid hair and a readable face.
- Cramp (f330–480): her right knee is up and both hands are on the calf, with a mesh-attached red pulse. Her left foot is planted on the floor.
- Window vignette (f826–920): the whale swims side-on past two spectators. The DA KRAS FILMS plate reads f760–1000 and the deck banner reads f1536–1736 (both well over 1.5 s). The tee logo shows in many shots.
- Grip (f1166–1330): her pointed foot is in the beluga's open mouth (pink lips).

## Remaining faults (film frames)
1. f921–996: the whale's flank fills the frame. The camera is too close as it passes the window.
2. f1126–1161: the whale sweeps past the lens and its back fills the frame while it turns up to her.
3. f1201–1330: static wide hold of about 2 s. She is small in a star pose and the whale is cropped at the bottom with its back to the camera.
4. f1340–1500: the whale leaves the frame, so "lifted by the beluga" doesn't read. Low angles at f1426–1466 are crotch-heavy.
5. f1501–1530: milky white surface with a mirrored ghost of her. Needs a darker surface from below and a quicker camera crossing.
6. f1536–1736 ending: her whole body shows through very clear water, so she reads as standing in a shallow pool. The whale's head is below her feet at f1561–1611. Little motion and no clear gasp or smile.
7. f251–316: a stiff upright float of about 1 s before the cramp.
8. The arm poses still look puppet-like (wide sculling) in the float shots.

## Suggested next round (if the director funds it)
- Ending: make the water from above opaque (deep teal, with fresnel and no transmission) so only her head and shoulders show, and put the whale's spy-hop clearly beside her. Animate the gasp and smile after f1540.
- Lift: one wider rising shot that keeps the whale and her face in frame (camera on the far side, lens 20, rising with them).
- Camera: keep at least 1.5 m from the whale at f920–1000 and f1120–1160.
